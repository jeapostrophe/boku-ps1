XCH.43	Narrator	A full twenty-five years have already slipped away since that summer. // Today I work at a certain major electronics manufacturer, // mostly writing control programs for communications equipment and drawing up designs. // White partitions and gray carpet; // the cold glow of fluorescent lights that never sleep, 24 hours a day. // In a dull, dry office, under a tight schedule, // I sometimes picture them: // how, bathed in strong sunlight, feeling the sweat run down my neck, // I ran all over the hills and fields... // those days of that summer.
XCH.44	Narrator	That was everything I remembered of that summer. // Afterwards I became a successful novelist, // and now I live in a hotel in New York with a view of the Brooklyn Bridge, // searching for the words of the epilogue that will adorn the end of this story of my childhood. // Come to think of it, for the countdown to 2001, // it seems Shirabe and Moe will be coming over from Japan too, for the first time in a long while. // Goodbye, 20th century.
XCH.45	Narrator	Within some 15 years of that summer vacation, // a huge multipurpose dam was completed in the Tsukiyono valley. // Around where my uncle's house once stood, // the water is 200 meters deep in the rainy season. // Nothing is left anymore to remember those days by.
# UNSURE XCH.45: the valley's name was misheard ('tsukiyo no tani', 'kiiro no tani'); read as
#   Tsukiyono (glossary § 2). 'Multipurpose dam' is 'tamokuteki damu', which the ASR garbled.
