# The insect book and the kite book (PLAN GFX-06): the English the build typesets onto the
# encyclopedia spreads, which the original has only as pixels. Insect pages are the 9 spreads
# of MZKAN0/MZKAN1 (the same 9 in both packs), kite pages the 8 spreads of TZKAN, each numbered
# in pack order. Format: translation/textures/README.md; where and how each field is set:
# boku/texture_books.py, research/texture-recipes.md § "The books". The name is set in the
# game's glyphs, everything else in the Bean face; a field that does not fit is refused.
#
# An insect page has a photo and, to its right, a header of four lines (name, family, size,
# what it likes) above a rule, then a body. The Japanese body starts under the rule and runs
# on under the photo, in vertical columns; the English runs down the left page, under the
# photo, and on to the right page, as an English book reads. Species names follow glossary 4a. A family is named in English after its best known
# member, as the Japanese names it; where that member is in glossary 4a, it is that name.
# The likes line sometimes names a place, not a food, because the source does.
#
# A kite page has the kite's brush lettered name, a difficulty (set in brackets by the build),
# and a body saying how to make or fly it. Kite names follow glossary 4d, less the hyphen the sheet lacks.

# --- Insect page 0: Musk Swallowtail. The caterpillars' food plant is spelled
# umanomizukusa on the page; the plant is umanosuzukusa, birthwort, and that is translated.
mzkan@0.name	Musk Swallowtail
mzkan@0.family	Swallowtail family
mzkan@0.size	Wingspan 100 to 120 mm
mzkan@0.food	Likes the nectar of flowers such as silverberry, Japanese thistle and azalea
mzkan@0.body	It does not like cold places and its food is limited, so in some regions it is a rare butterfly. It flies low and slowly over roadsides and grassy fields. The male's wings have a silky sheen, and it gives off a scent when caught, which is how it got the name Musk Swallowtail. The caterpillars eat birthwort, and they also have a strong habit of eating each other.

# --- Insect page 1: Mikado Swallowtail. Plants: tobera is Japanese pittosporum, yabugarashi
# bushkiller, ogatama no ki Michelia compressa, kept as the ogatama tree.
mzkan@1.name	Mikado Swallowtail
mzkan@1.family	Swallowtail family
mzkan@1.size	Wingspan 70 mm
mzkan@1.food	Likes the nectar of flowers such as Japanese pittosporum and bushkiller
mzkan@1.body	A common butterfly in places like Southeast Asia, but Japan is the northern edge of its range, and here it is scarce and has been named a Natural Monument in many places. The area around Ise Shrine is especially famous for it. Its caterpillars feed on the ogatama tree, a tree often planted in the grounds of shrines.

# --- Insect page 2: Peacock. Family tateha, the brushfoots, spelled without the usual
# hyphen. The wing sound is spelled shasha in the source, a mimetic word, so translated
# (style guide 7). Food line: the source word is filth, kept.
mzkan@2.name	Peacock
mzkan@2.family	Brushfoot family
mzkan@2.size	Wingspan 55 to 65 mm
mzkan@2.food	Likes the nectar of highland scabious and thistles, filth, and rotting fruit
mzkan@2.body	A butterfly with an unusual skill: when it takes off, for instance, it rubs its wings together and makes a swish, swish sound. It carries eyespots like a peacock's on its wings, which is how it got its name. It likes mountains and cold places, and is often found where it is fairly easy to see, such as on roads and on top of rocks.

# --- Insect page 3: Great Purple Emperor. The food line is read as treetops of mixed woods,
# and the sap of sawtooth oak and the like; it could also be read as the sap of both.
mzkan@3.name	Great Purple Emperor
mzkan@3.family	Brushfoot family
mzkan@3.size	Wingspan 100 to 120 mm
mzkan@3.food	Likes woodland treetops, and the sap of trees such as sawtooth oak
mzkan@3.body	A very beautiful butterfly that stands for the nation of Japan. As its name says, the male wears wings that shine bluish purple on its back. It flies high over the trees, so catching one in the air is very hard. Because it loves sap, the few chances to catch one come when it has landed on a tree and is feeding.

# --- Insect page 4: Loomis's Blue. Family shijimi, the gossamer wings, again without the
# hyphen. The food plant is ichiigashi, Quercus gilva, an evergreen oak.
mzkan@4.name	Loomis's Blue
mzkan@4.family	Gossamer wing family
mzkan@4.size	Wingspan 30 mm
mzkan@4.food	The caterpillars feed on the ichii oak, of the beech family
mzkan@4.body	Butterflies that have lived through the winter lay eggs in spring, and in early summer these emerge as new butterflies. It likes to perch on leaves, and on hot midsummer days it comes along the streams to drink water. Pesticides and typhoons have made it scarce, and it is a rare butterfly, named a Natural Monument on Mount Kasuga in Nara Prefecture.

# --- Insect page 5: Tengu Butterfly. Family named after it, as the Japanese is (English
# books say snout butterflies). Food plants: enoki is Chinese hackberry; the second is
# spelled ezonoki on the page, taken to be ezoenoki, Ezo hackberry, which the caterpillars eat.
mzkan@5.name	Tengu Butterfly
mzkan@5.family	Tengu Butterfly family
mzkan@5.size	Wingspan 40 to 50 mm
mzkan@5.food	The caterpillars eat Chinese hackberry and Ezo hackberry
mzkan@5.body	Its name comes from the small bump on its head, like a tengu's nose. The adults rest on roads, and on bare earth such as cliffs. It also has the curious habit of liking thin twigs and silver grass leaves. It goes dormant in midsummer, and spends the winter as an adult. In the cool seasons of spring and autumn, you can see it flying to flowers to drink nectar.

# --- Insect page 6: Green Skipper.
mzkan@6.name	Green Skipper
mzkan@6.family	Skipper family
mzkan@6.size	Wingspan 50 mm
mzkan@6.food	Likes all kinds of flowers, and places near wetlands
mzkan@6.body	A butterfly with very distinctive wings: deep green with orange spots. When a male finds a suitable spot, such as a clearing in the woods, it seems to have a habit of flying round and round the same course for a long time. Let's use this to catch it.

# --- Insect page 7: Ancient Dragonfly. The Himalayan species is capitalised as a common name,
# as every other species name in the book is. Two size lines in the source, body length then hind
# wing length, both about. Sanae is a clubtail, yanma a darner. The source writes the word
# for features with a wrong first character; the sense is not in doubt.
mzkan@7.name	Ancient Dragonfly
mzkan@7.family	Ancient Dragonfly family
mzkan@7.size	Body length about 50 mm, hind wing about 38 mm
mzkan@7.food	Likes the headwaters of mountain streams
mzkan@7.body	At first glance it looks like a clubtail or a small darner, but its eyes are set far apart, and the structure of its wings is like a damselfly's. Nearly 60 species of fossil dragonfly with the same features have been found, but the only living species are this one and the Himalayan Ancient Dragonfly. Truly a living fossil, and a very rare dragonfly.

# --- Insect page 8: Mountain Clubtail. Two size lines, as page 7. The Oniyanma family is
# the spiketails; named after glossary 4a's Oniyanma, as the families above are.
mzkan@8.name	Mountain Clubtail
mzkan@8.family	Clubtail family
mzkan@8.size	Body length 50 to 55 mm, hind wing 31 to 33 mm
mzkan@8.food	Likes ridgelines and places near mountaintops
mzkan@8.body	Its body has yellow and black stripes much like the Oniyanma family's, but what sets it apart is its long hind legs with spines like needles. It is born in the lower reaches of big rivers and in puddles on riverbeds, but right after it emerges it leaves the water's edge and lives on mountain ridges and near summits. In autumn it comes back down to the lowlands to lay its eggs, and ends its life.

# --- Kite page 0: Square Kite, shokyu. The body explains the fuller name, nakabari kakudako,
# so it is translated there as a name, Center Bow Square Kite (the sheet has no hyphen for
# middle bowed).
tzkan@0.name	Square Kite
tzkan@0.level	Beginner
tzkan@0.body	The second crosspiece from the top is given an especially strong bow, so it is called the Center Bow Square Kite. It is a kite that rises easily in a light wind. When it climbs unsteadily, put paper tails on its left and right corners.

# --- Kite page 1: Nina Kite, chukyu. The page does not say what nina means; it says only

tzkan@1.name	Nina Kite
tzkan@1.level	Intermediate
tzkan@1.body	A small kite for children, made on the island of Iki. Its spars are very thin, so fly it when the wind is light. Once it is up, its long red and white striped tails streaming in the wind stand out clearly, and it is beautiful.

# --- Kite page 2: Cicada Kite, chukyu.
tzkan@2.name	Cicada Kite
tzkan@2.level	Intermediate
tzkan@2.body	It is made with fairly thick spars, so fly it on a windy day. Give it a slightly stronger bow to make it three-dimensional. Flown in summer, it looks like a real cicada flying through the sky. Use a thin cord 2 to 3 m long for its tail.

# --- Kite page 3: Bird Kite, jokyu. Rittai dako, a kite with depth rather than a flat one,

tzkan@3.level	Advanced
tzkan@3.body	A kite built in three dimensions, in the shape of a bird with big wings. Its colorful orange body stands out against the blue sky, and it rises gracefully, as if swimming through the wide sky.

# --- Kite page 4: glossary 4d's Carp-streamer Kite, less the hyphen; jokyu.
tzkan@4.name	Carp Streamer Kite
tzkan@4.level	Advanced
tzkan@4.body	A kite built in three dimensions, in the shape of a carp streamer swimming cheerfully across the sky. Its frame is made from thin square sticks. It is bigger and heavier than other kites, so it takes a lot of strength to get it up, but after that it flies steadily.

# --- Kite page 5: Airplane Kite. The bracket is tatsujin, expert, not a kyu grade.
tzkan@5.name	Airplane Kite
tzkan@5.level	Expert
tzkan@5.body	A small, very steady kite built in three dimensions. Take care not to let it hit the ground hard when it lands. If you pull in the line well, you can bring it back to your hand while it is still flying.

# --- Kite page 6: Kite Train, tatsujin. The page calls the whole train one kite (tako de), so

tzkan@6.body	A kite made from light, colorful plastic bags. It goes from about 5 kites up to long ones of about 100, and it can stand anything from a light wind to a fairly strong one. Take care not to let the line break or let it get away.
