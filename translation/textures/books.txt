# The insect book and the kite book (PLAN GFX-06): the English the build typesets onto the
# encyclopedia spreads, which the original has only as pixels. Insect pages are the 9 spreads
# of MZKAN0/MZKAN1 (the same 9 in both packs), kite pages the 8 spreads of TZKAN, each numbered
# in pack order. Format: translation/textures/README.md; where and how each field is set:
# boku/texture_books.py, research/texture-recipes.md § "The books". The name is set in the
# game's glyphs, everything else in the Bean face; a field that does not fit is refused.
#
# An insect page has a photo and, to its right, a header of four lines (name, family, size,
# what it likes) above a rule, then a body. The Japanese body starts under the rule and runs
# on under the photo, in vertical columns; the English runs down the left page, under the
# photo, and on to the right page, as an English book reads. Species names follow glossary 4a.
# A family takes its usual English name where English has one (Swallowtail, Brushfoot,
# Gossamer-wing, Skipper, Clubtail); where it has none, it is named after its member, as the
# Japanese names it (Tengu Butterfly, Ancient Dragonfly).
# The likes line sometimes names a place, not a food, because the source does.
#
# A kite page has the kite's brush-lettered name, a difficulty (set in brackets by the build),
# and a body saying how to make or fly it. Kite names follow glossary 4d.

# --- Insect page 0: Musk Swallowtail. Plants: gumi is silverberry (Elaeagnus), noazami the Japanese thistle, tsutsuji azalea. The caterpillars' food plant reads "umanomizukusa" on the page (checked against the image); the plant is umanosuzukusa (Aristolochia), birthwort, and that is translated.
mzkan@0.name	Musk Swallowtail
mzkan@0.family	Swallowtail family
mzkan@0.size	Wingspan 100 to 120 mm
mzkan@0.food	Likes the nectar of flowers such as silverberry, Japanese thistle and azalea
mzkan@0.body	It does not like cold places and its food is limited, so in some regions it is a rare butterfly. It flies low and slowly over roadsides and grassy fields. The male's wings have a silky sheen, and it gives off a scent when caught, which is how it got the name Musk Swallowtail. The caterpillars eat birthwort, and they are also prone to eating each other.

# --- Insect page 1: Mikado Swallowtail. Plants: tobera is Japanese pittosporum (Pittosporum tobira) and yabugarashi bushkiller (Cayratia japonica); ogatama no ki (Michelia compressa) has no common English name, so it is the ogatama tree. Ise Jingu is the Ise Grand Shrine, its usual English name.
mzkan@1.name	Mikado Swallowtail
mzkan@1.family	Swallowtail family
mzkan@1.size	Wingspan 70 mm
mzkan@1.food	Likes the nectar of flowers such as Japanese pittosporum and bushkiller
mzkan@1.body	It is a common butterfly in places like Southeast Asia, but in Japan, the northern edge of its range, there are few of them, and it has been named a natural monument in many places. The area around the Ise Grand Shrine is especially famous as a place to find it. The caterpillars' food plant is the ogatama tree. This is a tree often planted in the grounds of shrines and such places.

# --- Insect page 2: Peacock. The family, tateha-cho, is the brushfoots (Nymphalidae), their English name; tateha is not one species, and "Admiral family" would name a group English books do not have. Plants: matsumushisou is a scabious, called pincushion flower in English gardens; azami thistle; the likes line is read as the nectar of both, then the source's word for filth -- on a butterfly's menu, dung -- then rotting fruit. The wing sound is spelled shasha in quotation marks in the source, a mimetic word, so translated (style guide 7); the sheet has no double quote.
mzkan@2.name	Peacock
mzkan@2.family	Brushfoot family
mzkan@2.size	Wingspan 55 to 65 mm
mzkan@2.food	Likes the nectar of highland pincushion flowers and thistles, dung, and rotting fruit
mzkan@2.body	A butterfly with an unusual skill: when it takes off, for instance, it rubs its wings together and makes a swish, swish sound. It carries eyespots like a peacock's on its wings, which is how it got its name. It likes mountains and cold places, and is often found where it is fairly easy to see, such as on roads and on top of rocks.

# --- Insect page 3: Great Purple Emperor. Family as page 2. "As its name says" refers to murasaki, purple, which the English name also has. The likes line is the treetops of mixed woods (zokibayashi), where it lives, and the sap of trees such as the sawtooth oak (kunugi, glossary 7).
mzkan@3.name	Great Purple Emperor
mzkan@3.family	Brushfoot family
mzkan@3.size	Wingspan 100 to 120 mm
mzkan@3.food	Likes the treetops of mixed woods and the sap of trees such as the sawtooth oak
mzkan@3.body	A very beautiful butterfly that stands for the nation of Japan. As its name says, the male wears shining bluish-purple wings on its back. It flies high over the trees, so catching one in the air is very hard. Because it loves sap, the few chances to catch one come when it has landed on a tree and is feeding.

# --- Insect page 4: Loomis's Blue. The family, shijimi-cho, is the gossamer-winged butterflies (Lycaenidae), their English name. The food plant is ichiigashi (Quercus gilva), an evergreen oak with no common English name, kept as the ichii oak (gashi is oak); buna-ka is the beech family. Kasugayama in Nara Prefecture is Mount Kasuga.
mzkan@4.name	Loomis's Blue
mzkan@4.family	Gossamer-wing family
mzkan@4.size	Wingspan 30 mm
mzkan@4.food	The caterpillars feed on the ichii oak, of the beech family
mzkan@4.body	Butterflies that have lived through the winter lay eggs in spring, and in early summer these emerge as new butterflies. It likes to perch on leaves, and on hot midsummer days it comes to the creeks to drink water. Pesticides and typhoons have made it scarce, and it is a rare butterfly, named a Natural Monument on Mount Kasuga in Nara Prefecture.

# --- Insect page 5: Tengu Butterfly. Family named after it, as the Japanese is (English books say snout butterflies). Food plants: enoki is Chinese hackberry (Celtis sinensis); the second reads "ezonoki" on the page (checked against the image), taken to be ezo-enoki, the Ezo hackberry (Celtis jessoensis), which the caterpillars eat. The adults rest on the earth of roads, cliffs and the like: the ground. Susuki is Japanese silver grass.
mzkan@5.name	Tengu Butterfly
mzkan@5.family	Tengu Butterfly family
mzkan@5.size	Wingspan 40 to 50 mm
mzkan@5.food	The caterpillars eat Chinese hackberry and Ezo hackberry
mzkan@5.body	Its name comes from the small bump on its head, like a tengu's nose. Adults rest on the ground, such as on roads or cliffs. They also have an interesting fondness for thin twigs and silver grass leaves. It goes dormant in midsummer and lives through the winter as an adult. In the cool of spring and autumn, you can see it flying to flowers to drink nectar.

# --- Insect page 7: Ancient Dragonfly. The Himalayan species is capitalised as a common name, as every other species name in the book is. Two size lines in the source, body length then hind wing length, both about. Sanae is a clubtail, yanma a darner, ito-tonbo a damselfly. The source writes the word for features with a wrong first character; the sense is not in doubt.
mzkan@7.name	Ancient Dragonfly
mzkan@7.family	Ancient Dragonfly family
mzkan@7.size	Body length about 50 mm, hind wing about 38 mm
mzkan@7.food	Likes the headwaters of mountain streams
mzkan@7.body	At first glance it looks like a clubtail or a small darner, but its eyes are set far apart, and the structure of its wings is like a damselfly's. Nearly 60 species of fossil dragonfly with the same features have been found, but the only living species are this one and the Himalayan Ancient Dragonfly. Truly a living fossil, and a very rare dragonfly.

# --- Insect page 8: Mountain Clubtail. Two size lines, as page 7. The Oniyanma family is the spiketails; named after glossary 4a's Oniyanma.
mzkan@8.name	Mountain Clubtail
mzkan@8.family	Clubtail family
mzkan@8.size	Body length 50 to 55 mm, hind wing 31 to 33 mm
mzkan@8.food	Likes ridgelines and places near mountaintops
mzkan@8.body	Its body has yellow and black stripes much like the Oniyanma family's, but it stands out for its long hind legs with needle-like spines. It is born in places such as the lower reaches of big rivers and riverbed puddles, but leaves the water's edge right after it emerges, to live on mountain ridges and near summits. In autumn it returns to the lowlands to lay its eggs, and ends its life.

The body explains the fuller name, naka-bari kaku-dako (the second crosspiece bowed tight, "stretched in the middle"), so it is translated there as a name, Center-bow Square Kite; the dialogue (E0710.16) says only Square Kite.
tzkan@0.name	Square Kite
tzkan@0.level	Beginner
tzkan@0.body	The second crosspiece from the top is given an especially strong bow, so it is called the Center-bow Square Kite. It is a kite that rises easily in a light wind. When it climbs unsteadily, put paper tails on its left and right corners.
