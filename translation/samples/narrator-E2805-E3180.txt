# DRAFT sample — an emotional scene with the adult narrator.
# E2805: day 28 (August 28), night. Boku has come down with a fever at dinner (E2803); his aunt
# sits by his futon in the upstairs room (maps I30 / G14). The "boy who used to live here" is
# the Soranos' son, who died three years ago and is never given a name in the script: he is
# only ever "onii-chan", the family's word for their eldest boy.
# E3179-E3180: day 31, the front of the house. Boku's father has come to take him home.
# Policies exercised: QUESTIONS Q1 (Onii-chan), Q3 (narrator's voice), style-guide § Invented
# mimetics, § Recurring phrases ("the rule at Auntie's house").

E2805.0	Aunt	So you do have a fever, I wonder... Boku-kun, are you feeling bad right now?
E2805.1	Boku	Yeah... a little... kind of hohe-hohe.
E2805.2	Aunt	Two hohes, hm? Excuse me a moment...
E2805.3	Aunt	Your fever has gone up quite a bit. I'm sorry I didn't notice sooner... // Anyway, you've had your medicine for today, so let's get you off to sleep.
E2805.4	Boku	Hey, Auntie...
E2805.5	Aunt	Hm? What is it?
E2805.6	Boku	The boy who used to live at your house... I think he must have been really, really happy.
E2805.7	Aunt	Hm-hm... What makes you think so?
E2805.8	Boku	Do you think I... could take his place?
E2805.9	Aunt	...Oh? Hm-hm... // Onii-chan is Onii-chan. Boku-kun is Boku-kun. // But Auntie loves Boku-kun very much, too.
E2805.10	Boku	I love you too, Auntie.
E2805.11	Aunt	Hm-hm... Thank you. Well then, good night.
E2805.12	Narrator	In that moment, for some reason, I wanted with all my heart... // to curl up small and round, like a cat in love with a patch of sun on a spring day... // and to be held, gently, in my aunt's arms...
E2805.13	Boku	Auntie...
E2805.14	Aunt	Hm? What's the matter?
E2805.15	Boku	...Good night.
E2805.16	Aunt	Yes... Good night.
E2805.17	Aunt	Incidentally, tonight's dinner was cabbage rolls.

# NOTES
# * The narrator says watashi and "my aunt" (oba, the plain kinship word); the child says boku
#   and "Oba-chan". The English keeps them apart the same way: the narrator is "I" with a full,
#   written sentence and a simile; the child is short and spoken. The narrator never says
#   "Auntie". (style-guide § The narrator.)
# * E2805.12 pays off E2805.8-.11: he asks for the hug on day 31 (E3179.6). The narrator's verb
#   and the child's are different words in the Japanese too; "be held" / "Hold me" is as close
#   as English lets them come, and the echo is wanted.
# * E3180.0 is the last use of a phrase the family has used on Boku all month for every
#   prohibition — "that's the rule at Uncle's house" (day 1 E0185.0, day 3 E0303.3, the night
#   gate E0002.1). It must be word-for-word the same frame every time or this line does not
#   land. It is a glossary entry.
# * E2805.17 is the dinner-quiz answer, which the fever made Boku miss. The bathos is original.
# * "hohe-hohe" is not a Japanese word; Boku makes it up, and the aunt counts the syllables. A
#   made-up sound stays as the sound (style-guide § Invented mimetics). A real mimetic word
#   would be translated.
# * "Hm-hm" is the aunt's closed-mouth laugh (fufu). See style-guide § Laughter for the table
#   and the alternative.
# * "I love you too" — the aunt says daisuki, Boku answers with the plainer suki. "I like you
#   too, Auntie" is the literal alternative; it reads colder in English than it is in Japanese.
