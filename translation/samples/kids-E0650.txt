# NOTES
# * E0650.11 — in the Japanese the quoted English word is followed by a parenthesised Japanese
#   gloss for the player's benefit. Boku still doesn't get it (.13). In English the gloss would
#   gloss a word into itself, so it is dropped: nothing is lost, the reader is simply already
#   where the Japanese player was put. This is the one class of "cut" the style guide allows
#   (§ English inside the Japanese) and it is flagged every time it happens. Jay's exception
#   (Q10.6, 2026-09-20) — a gloss that is "characteristic and funny for the character" may
#   stay — does not apply here: this gloss is a player aid in the text, not the boys' words.
# * "yours truly" is Fat's pronoun: he says ore-sama ("my august self") every time. Guts is plain
#   ore; Megane is a careful, bookish boku and calls people kimi. English has one "I", so the
#   difference is carried by diction: Fat swaggers, Guts is curt, Megane talks like a book.
# * "beansprout" is moyashikko, the period insult for a pale, weedy city child. Literal, and it
#   reads. Fat uses it again on day 29 (E2952.0) — glossary.
# * Guts and the monk call him Boku-chan; the family and Megane say Boku-kun; Saori coins
#   "Boku-kun" as a joke when he introduces himself (E1861). The suffix is characterisation,
#   and the player can hear it, so it stays.
# * "Megane" means "glasses". Kept as heard (Q4, ruled 2026-09-20); "Specs" was the rejected
#   alternative.
# * .11 has no speaker label in the Japanese; its speaker operand is group 15 (Guts + Fat +
#   Megane, research/event-scripts.md § Speakers) — all three deliver the punchline together, a
#   story they have plainly heard many times. The label here is ours.
